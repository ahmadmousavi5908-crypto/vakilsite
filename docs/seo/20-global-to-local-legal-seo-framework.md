# Global-to-Local Legal SEO Framework
Date: 2026-11-25
Site: https://vakilahmadmousavi.ir/
Execution market: Ahvaz → Khuzestan → Iran
Principle: Global operating standards, local legal relevance.

## Strategic decision
Adopt the operating model of strong US and European legal practices, not their visual styling alone. Transfer practice-area architecture, attorney identity and verifiable credentials, legal knowledge hubs, conversion paths, mobile usability, editorial governance, and measurement. Localize every implementation to Persian search behavior, Iranian law and procedure, Khuzestan jurisdictional context, and services the office actually provides.

International benchmarking is a source of hypotheses and quality standards, not proof that copying a design guarantees rankings. No ranking, traffic, or client outcome is guaranteed.

## Evidence and limitations
- Undermind's review "Law firm digital acquisition conversion trust and local search systems" surfaced 83 relevant sources. Most summaries rely on abstracts/metadata; full texts of Hag20 and Rog23 were read.
- Hagan & Li (2020), DOI 10.2139/ssrn.3623333: preliminary audit of 1,400 Google searches from five locations, producing 18,824 result entries. Many recurring legal-help results were generic and insufficiently jurisdiction-specific. Observational, limited to Google and a small set of US locations in 2019; it does not prove a ranking factor or causal effect of schema/content changes.
- Rogers & Song (2023), DOI 10.5204/lthj.2828: review of legal digital marketing, not a controlled SEO experiment. It identifies benefits and risks involving misleading advertising, confidentiality, privacy, cybersecurity, and unintended lawyer-client expectations.
- Peretto & Pisati (2018), DOI 10.1425/91629: a 64-participant profile experiment found educational qualifications and peer reputation affected perceived lawyer trustworthiness. Small sample; not evidence of Google ranking effects.
- US literature is more visible than European evidence in this review. No rigorous cross-country study proves that a specific US/EU website pattern will produce the same ranking effect in Iran.

## Transferable international operating patterns
1. Practice-area architecture: one clear parent page per genuine practice area. Create child URLs only for distinct intent and sufficient original value.
2. Attorney entity and credentials: consistent name, accurate professional title, verifiable bar/license details, real education, actual practice areas, authored/reviewed work, and clear contact routes.
3. Legal knowledge hub: problem-led explainers, legislation and judgment analysis, checklists, natural FAQs, update dates, primary sources, and links to the relevant service page.
4. Jurisdiction-first content: distinguish national law from local procedure. Include local courts, authorities, deadlines, and process only when verified and useful.
5. Conversion by service, not pressure: prominent mobile phone/contact options, short privacy-conscious forms, realistic response expectations, confidentiality notes, and a clear statement that submitting a form does not itself create a retainer.
6. Trust governance: build authority through real publications, verifiable professional profiles, consistent facts, and responsible editorial review. No fabricated reviews, case results, rankings, or guarantees.
7. Technical quality: crawlable HTML, mobile usability, accessibility, performance, canonical, sitemap, metadata, accurate structured data, and contextual internal links. Structured data does not guarantee rankings or rich results.
8. Measurement: GSC query/page/device/country, GA4 contact events, answered calls, qualified inquiries, and consultations. Visits alone are not business success.

## Iran and Khuzestan localization
- Write in natural Persian. Pair colloquial problem descriptions with legally precise terminology.
- Ahvaz pages must include genuine local value. Expand to Abadan, Khorramshahr, Dezful, Shushtar, Andimeshk, Mahshahr and other Khuzestan cities only where services and distinct local content justify it. No city-name doorway pages.
- Foreign examples are UX and operating references, never sources for Iranian substantive law.
- Verify Iranian law, competent authority, and current procedure before publication.
- Professional advertising rules and client confidentiality override any marketing tactic.
- Borrow information architecture, clarity, educational depth, conversion design, and quality controls; never copy competitors' text, bios, data, reviews, or claims.

## Execution priorities
### P0: Existing service and conversion paths
- Keep the property service page as the owner of commercial intent such as property-lawyer/services in Ahvaz.
- Keep "difference between eviction, recovery of possession, and unlawful occupation" informational; link naturally to the property service page. Avoid intent cannibalization.
- Test phone, WhatsApp, and forms end-to-end on mobile. Validate contact_click in GA4 before marking it as a key event.
- Inspect live HTML, canonical, title/description, OG, and indexability URL by URL. Do not mass-rewrite metadata based on one potentially faulty audit parser.

### P1: Global-grade information architecture
- Improve the lawyer profile and verifiable credentials without unsupported claims or unapproved UI redesign.
- Strengthen parent service pages for criminal, family, property, and cheque/debt matters according to real services and distinct search intent.
- Each service page should explain: who it helps, problems covered, general process/documents where legally safe, boundaries, real FAQs, sources, and a clear contact path.
- Build a connected legal knowledge hub that supports service pages through contextual internal links.

### P2: Local and national authority
- Keep business name, address, and phone consistent across eligible trusted profiles.
- Connect genuine academic publications and professional profiles with verifiable attribution.
- Earn relevant editorial references from legal, academic, and local sources. No manipulative paid links.
- Expand Khuzestan city coverage only with genuine local differentiation and actual service coverage.

### P3: Experiment and scale
- Record a baseline before each change; ship small, attributable changes.
- Review GSC query/page and qualified contact outcomes after crawl and over comparable 28/56-day windows. Small impression samples do not support confident conclusions.
- Stop rollout and investigate root cause if technical health or conversions deteriorate.

## Success measures
1. Growth in non-brand Iran queries, especially Ahvaz/Khuzestan clusters.
2. Organic clicks to service pages, not only the homepage.
3. More answered calls and qualified inquiries from organic search.
4. Mobile Core Web Vitals based on valid CrUX/PSI evidence.
5. No intent cannibalization, doorway pages, thin duplicates, or unsupported claims.

## Hard constraints
- No guaranteed first position, fixed timeline, or unsupported "best lawyer" claim.
- No copied content, credentials, reviews, or case outcomes.
- No city pages created by swapping place names.
- Do not infer causality from correlation or observational studies.
- Never apply US search volume, KD, CPC, or rankings to Iran.
- Preserve approved UI and Mother Two Updated Mavis. Production changes require branch/PR, CI, deployment, and smoke-test evidence.
- Human legal review remains mandatory before publishing legal content.
